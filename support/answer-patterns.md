# Answer patterns

Recurring questions and situations with the approved answer logic, so a new DM, comment, or email can reuse what Tania has already decided. Search here before drafting. Adjust for the person in front of you; never copy a reply when conditions differ. Each entry: situation, who it affects, approved logic, sample wording, exceptions and escalation, source, last used. "Last used" is unknown for all entries until Shadia supplies real examples. Status: confirmed logic from the source; no live examples yet.

## AP-01: "They told me not to contact them. Should I stop?"

- Affects: any parent after a no-contact, "don't contact me," or space request, even a harsh one.
- Approved logic: respect it by reaching less often, not never. Real space first (weeks, not days), then the next short, light, no-pressure touchpoint on a stretched cadence (for example every couple of months). Waiting entirely for them to reopen the window can mean waiting forever.
- Sample: "I know you need space right now. I'll give that to you. Just want you to know I love you and I'm still here when you're ready."
- Exceptions: none in source. Repeat mistake: never tell a parent to go fully silent or "let it sit."
- Source: KB §4 checklist item 2, §5.

## AP-02: "I reached out and got anger back. It made things worse."

- Approved logic: a sharp first reply after long silence is activity where there was none; the child's nervous system responded. Often the initial flare, not the final word. A reaction is not a verdict. Continue on a steady rhythm that asks nothing back; escalation often means the message touched something real, so more depth with less pressure and a longer runway.
- Sample: "I know that conversation was heavy. I just want you to know I'm here, and I'm listening." Then 2 to 3 weeks before re-engaging.
- Source: KB §1.5.8, §5, Challenge Day 51.

## AP-03: "My child called me toxic / a narcissist / unsafe / said I ruined their childhood."

- Approved logic: never defend against the label. Don't accept it, argue it, ask where it came from, or send proof. Decode the need underneath (see [../brand/decode-library.md](../brand/decode-library.md)), answer the pain briefly, let consistent calm behavior be the argument.
- Sample: "I don't remember it that way, but I can hear how painful that was for you. I'm sorry you felt that."
- Source: KB §5 master rule.

## AP-04: "What do I actually say?" / "How often should I send?"

- Approved logic: point to the 100+ Scripts bundle in the classroom by category name and number, plus one personalized sentence for their situation. Rhythm by situation: cold estrangement every 3 to 4 weeks; warm estrangement every 7 to 10 days; teen or very young adult 1 to 2 weeks; minor living elsewhere once or twice a week. Full table in the decode library.
- Sample: "This is covered in the 100+ Scripts bundle in the classroom, category 3 (re-openers after silence), worth downloading and reviewing. For your situation I'd keep it to one line, something like..."
- Source: KB §6, §5 pacing, Challenge Day 41.

## AP-05: "Should I even keep reaching out? I'm done." (Empty Tank, Doubt Loop, "I'm done" stage)

- Approved logic: name it as a stage, not a fuel gauge; refill first; keep the rhythm steady when the feeling wavers; calendar the send dates; never decide the relationship from inside the numbness. The exhaustion is real and human, and the question is where the reaching comes from (fear vs confidence).
- Source: KB §12, Challenge Day 43. Premium members can be pointed to Hot Seat; non-Premium cannot (tier rule).

## AP-06: Grandparent access, rules, supervised visits

- Approved logic: comply with the adult child's rules without visible reluctance; a condition is usually them working out how to keep you in; never disagree with the adult child in front of the grandchildren; keep grandkid touches light and about that child; never go around them. Every easy visit removes a reason for the rule.
- Source: KB §5, §12 (Grandkids as the Gate), scripts category 13.

## AP-07: Partner, in-law, ex, or therapist "wedge"

- Approved logic: the child goes home to that person every night. Never criticize the third party, never ask the child to defend them, never send proof or relitigate. Be so calm and consistent that direct experience of you stops matching the story. Contact with the child is about the child only. "You can't compete, but you can create contrast."
- Source: KB §5, §12, Challenge Day 38, scripts category 10.

## AP-08: Excluded from a wedding, graduation, birth

- Approved logic: the event is not the verdict. Do not campaign for a seat, do not disappear. One warm no-ask signal that leaves the door open. Carry the grief sideways with your own people. Do not pre-load an upcoming event into a message ("looking forward to seeing you there") unless the child signaled warmth first or the setting is private.
- Sample (new baby): "Congratulations on your beautiful baby. Wishing you both health, peace, and rest during this special time."
- Source: KB §12, §4 (forward-looking statements), §5.

## AP-09: Blocked on every channel

- Approved logic: a block is a boundary, not a verdict. Stop forcing a channel; never go around it through others. Keep a private practice of presence (bank the lines, a card where a door is still open), build your own life, hold the door from your side.
- Source: KB §12 (The Blocked Wall), Challenge Days 25 and 41.

## AP-10: "This doesn't sound like me / it feels fake."

- Approved logic: "I love your directness, I always appreciate honesty." Connect where true. This is about speaking the child's language, not becoming fake. Relationship over style. Over time it becomes their own voice.
- Source: KB §4.

## AP-11: "You're blaming the parent" / "victim shaming"

- Approved logic: ground in Tania's authority (she lived the child's side), reframe as one question (how can you show up in a way that has a real chance of changing the dynamic), say plainly it is not about excusing anyone or blaming them, reclaim power, validate the hurt, close by naming what they already did right.
- Source: KB §4.

## AP-12: "How do I work with you?" / asks for an appointment

- Approved logic: story before offer. Get who they are estranged from and how long. Then route to the community, or the soft VIP open ("Let me know if you wanted to chat more about it"). Full offer only after a yes. Never lead with price or a paid link. Never promise reconnection. The one promise: "You'd stop guessing."
- Source: KB §9. Details in [../offers/premium-and-vip.md](../offers/premium-and-vip.md).

## AP-13: Premium client asks for more 1:1 calls or messages daily

- Approved logic: Premium is one Kickoff plus one 90-Day Reset, plus chat, voice notes, and the monthly group calls. Do not create a monthly 1:1 habit; redirect warmly as abundance. For near-daily messaging, frame a once-a-week "gather your thoughts and bring them to me" rhythm as part of their growth, keeping access open for anything urgent.
- Source: KB §10 (Tania, 2026-08-20 and 2026-08-24).

## AP-14: A member compliments Tania directly

- Approved logic: redirect it onto them. "I appreciate the thank you, but this one's yours."
- Source: KB §4.

## AP-15: First introduction post

- Approved logic: warmth only, reflect what they shared, "we're here to support you, I look forward to getting to know you and seeing you on the live calls." No resource routing.
- Source: KB §4.

## AP-16: Member in acute crisis or reporting the work leaves them low for days

- Approved logic: this is a capacity limit, not resistance. Hold and pace: explicit permission to set the work down without guilt, point to their own therapist for heavy days, re-invite later. Do not push empowerment on someone who is crashing.
- Escalation: Olivia does not create crisis content. Flag to Shadia.
- Source: KB §1.5.13.

## AP-17: "Have things changed between us? I don't feel the support like before."

- Approved logic: an attachment bid. Answer it first and directly (nothing has changed, you have not lost me), validate the feeling without taking the blame ("I am glad you told me how it felt").
- Source: KB §1.5.13.

## AP-18: Child demands a confession or a list of what the parent did wrong

- Approved logic: never confess to what did not happen. Name the one specific true thing and apologize for that alone, then stop. Never ask the child for a list; find the pattern and apologize to the pattern. Assume one specific apology rather than asking "what did I do."
- Source: KB §5, §14d.

## AP-19: Young adult comes home with a new worldview that frames the parent as harmful

- Approved logic: ordinary individuation with a ready-made explanation attached. Don't debate; get curious ("where did you hear that," "what changed your mind"); state a differing view once and stop.
- Source: KB §5.

## AP-20: Occasional or "crumbs" contact, parent despairing

- Approved logic: name it as distance, not estrangement. The door is narrow, not closed. Keep the rhythm steadier than in a full cutoff (a 6-week gap is too long), take natural landmarks, and don't over-apply the "big gesture equals pressure" caution when the child still engages.
- Source: KB §1.5.11c (Tania, 2026-09-07).
